\section{1. Dual-MCU System Architecture (Safety Reflex + AI Vision)}
To maintain the safety guarantees demonstrated in Phase 1 while adding real-time object identification for the finals, we employ a \textbf{two-microcontroller distributed architecture}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Primary Safety Controller (ESP32-C3 SuperMini):} Handles safety-critical, 100\% offline reflexes in a sub-50\,ms loop: forward ultrasonic ranging, handle vibration PWM (200\,Hz), silent fall/tilt detection via MPU6050, and Bluetooth Low Energy (BLE) telemetry to the smartphone. It never halts for network handshakes.
    \item \textbf{Secondary Vision Controller (ESP32-CAM + OV2640):} Acts as an on-demand AI camera coprocessor. Triggered when obstacles enter the 1\,m warning boundary or upon user voice command. It captures JPEG snapshots and streams them via Wi-Fi to the companion Android phone for cloud/local object recognition and audio feedback.
\end{itemize}

\section{2. Power Distribution Architecture \& Brownout Prevention}
\textbf{The Engineering Challenge:} The ESP32-CAM draws heavy transient current spikes of \textbf{450\,mA -- 600\,mA} during Wi-Fi transmission bursts and camera sensor readout. If powered from a weak 3.3\,V rail or an under-rated boost regulator, supply voltage sags below 4.5\,V, causing the onboard AMS1117-3.3 regulator to drop out and trigger an immediate MCU brownout panic reset.

\vspace{0.3em}
\noindent\textbf{Power Supply Pipeline \& Schematic Topology:}
\begin{center}
\small
\fbox{\textbf{18650 Li-ion Cell}} (3.7\,V, 2600\,mAh) 
$\longrightarrow$ \fbox{\textbf{TP4056 (DW01A)}} (Type-C Charger + Protection) 
$\longrightarrow$ \fbox{\textbf{SPST Switch}} 
$\longrightarrow$ \fbox{\textbf{MT3608 Boost}} (5.0\,V 2\,A) 
$\longrightarrow$ \fbox{\textbf{1000\,$\mu$F Low-ESR Cap}} 
$\longrightarrow$ \textbf{5\,V Common Rail}
\end{center}

\vspace{-0.4em}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Common 5\,V Rail:} Both the ESP32-C3 (via \texttt{5V} pin) and ESP32-CAM (via \texttt{5V} pin) are powered from the boosted 5.0\,V line. Their internal LDOs step this down cleanly to 3.3\,V without mutual interference.
    \item \textbf{1000\,$\mu$F Decoupling Reservoir:} A 1000\,$\mu$F / 16\,V Low-ESR electrolytic capacitor is soldered directly across the 5\,V and GND pins of the ESP32-CAM. This acts as an immediate local charge reservoir, buffering 500\,mA RF bursts and preventing voltage dips.
    \item \textbf{Power Budget \& Runtime:} Continuous current is $\approx 400$\,mA @ 5\,V ($\approx 2.0$\,W). A single genuine 2600\,mAh cell provides \textbf{$>$4.0 hours} of continuous streaming and guidance. Putting the camera into Light Sleep between scans extends operation to \textbf{$>$8 hours}.
\end{itemize}

\section{5. Critical Assembly Rules \& Verification Steps}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Calibrate MT3608 Before Plugging Anything In:} MT3608 trimpots often ship set to 15--20\,V. Connect the 3.7\,V battery and turn the brass screw counter-clockwise while probing with a digital multimeter until the output reads \textbf{exactly 5.0\,V}. Only then solder it to the MCU rails.
    \item \textbf{Power ESP32-CAM Through 5\,V Pin (Never 3.3\,V):} Always feed the regulated 5.0\,V rail into the \texttt{5V} pin. The onboard AMS1117 regulator requires at least 4.75\,V input to produce a clean 3.3\,V output for the camera sensor.
    \item \textbf{Install 1000\,$\mu$F Capacitor at the Camera Header:} Solder the capacitor right between the \texttt{5V} and \texttt{GND} pins of the ESP32-CAM socket (observing polarity: negative stripe to GND).
    \item \textbf{Keep Onboard Flash LED OFF in Firmware:} The bright white flash LED on the ESP32-CAM is tied to \texttt{GPIO 4}. Keep it \texttt{LOW} in setup (\texttt{pinMode(4, OUTPUT); digitalWrite(4, LOW);}) so it does not draw 150\,mA of parasitic heat.
    \item \textbf{Common Ground:} Ensure the ESP32-C3 ground, ESP32-CAM ground, TP4056 ground, and boost converter ground are soldered into a unified star ground plane.
\end{enumerate}
